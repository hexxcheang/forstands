Plan 1 · recommended

Open on both long sides: a centre spine

The three modules bolt into one 3 m wall down the middle of the booth. Side A carries the headline products; Side B carries the rest of the range, so both aisles get a complete story. Your lock stand closes one end, with the outside of the lock to Side A and the inside screen to Side B. The counter closes the other end.

Each side has 967 mm of booth floor in front of the faces, and visitors also stand in the public aisle. The 300 mm of floor plate that reaches into each side is flush steel with a bevelled edge, so people stand on it rather than around it.

Plan 2 · same kit

One long side against a wall: three fins, four bays

If the back of the booth is a wall or a neighbour, turn the modules 90° so they stand as fins from the back. Every face then looks sideways into a bay about 1.1 m wide, and the lit end caps face the aisle as signposts. Nothing is built twice: the modules, cassettes and fabric are the same as Plan 1.

Bay 1 is the entry: visitors read the outside of the lock from the aisle, then step behind it to see the inside screen next to the smart lock face and the live S1 panel. Staff reach the counter by walking past the front of M3.

Why it lasts more than one show

  • Knock-down aluminium frame with corner connectors. No glue, no screws into the venue.
  • Fabric skins fold into a bag and reprint cheaply when a campaign changes.
  • Cassettes travel in their own foam-lined case, so switches never carry the frame's weight.
  • End caps are frosted acrylic over Lumi LED strip, removable when modules bolt side by side.
  • The 200 mm body leaves 64 mm clear for wiring between two 50 mm back boxes.

Standing it up safely

  • Floor plate 1,000 × 800 × 8 mm steel with levelling feet, so the base is four times the body depth.
  • In Plan 1 the three modules bolt into one spine. In Plan 2 a rear tie beam joins the three canopies.
  • Ask the fabricator for a push test at the top of each module before the first show.
  • All 230 V wiring by a licensed electrical worker, on RCD-protected strips inside each module.

Five things to confirm

Which sides are openBoth long sides open means Plan 1. A wall or neighbour behind means Plan 2.
Your lock standsSize and how many. They are drawn here as one stand of 900 × 300 mm; send the real size and the plans get redrawn around it.
Height limitThe kit is 2.4 m. Some malls cap roadshow booths lower, so check the organiser's manual.
PowerFour 13 A points are ideal, one per module and one for the counter. One point can work with a distribution board run by a licensed electrical worker.
Offers at the eventThe site lists a free S8 panel with qualifying packages at the showroom, and a free S1 with any F3 Ultra and a Premium Core package. Confirm both apply at the booth before printing.
