Build notes

What the fabricator needs to know

Top

  • 1,400 × 600, 150 mm corner radius, 40 mm thick edge.
  • Marble-look sintered stone or quartz, 12 mm on a plywood core with a mitred 40 mm edge, so it stays light enough to lift.
  • Overhangs the body by 30 mm all round. It lifts off for transport.

Body and shelves

  • Plywood with a champagne laminate, matching the booth's carpentry wall.
  • Closed centre, 760 wide, with the wordmark in cut bronze or printed vinyl.
  • Two open bays at each end, following the corner curve. The base and top boards are champagne laminate.
  • The middle shelf is solid oak, 40 mm thick, at 480–520 mm. It continues as a 16 mm oak band across the front and back of the centre, so it reads as one line.
  • No posts: the oak shelves are fixed into the centre with hidden steel brackets, which a 40 mm shelf can carry for display items.

Light

  • Lumi LED strip in an aluminium channel with a diffuser, set 20 mm back under the top edge, all the way round.
  • A second strip under the base, behind a 60 mm set-back, lighting the floor.
  • Warm white, on the Maxsen gateway, so the table joins the booth's scenes.

Power and use

  • One 13 A point. The cable enters through the base, and the driver sits inside the closed centre.
  • 1 m is a standing counter height. The body fills the space under the top, so there is no knee room for sitting.
  • The closed centre opens from the back as a lockable cupboard for the iPad, brochures and bags.
Height

Is 1 metre too high? No, for this table

The shelves and the closed centre fill the space under the top, so nobody can sit with their knees under it. That makes it a standing counter, and 1,000 mm is the standard height for one: a visitor can rest an arm or an iPad on it while talking to staff face to face.
