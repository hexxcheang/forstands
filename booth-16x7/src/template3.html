The big L

Carpentry wall and fabric lightbox

The carpentry wall is one-sided: the products face into the booth and the back faces the neighbour. It is built as four panels of about 1,190 mm so it breaks down for the next show. The lightbox gives the booth its glow and carries the brand line.

Fabric lightbox

  • 2,134 × 1,800 × 120 mm aluminium frame along the full 7 ft side. About 1,980 mm of lit face shows beside the carpentry wall.
  • Printed fabric with a silicone edge (SEG) pushed into the frame, lit from behind by LED modules on a dimmer. Run it at about 65%, so it reads as atmosphere rather than signage.
  • Artwork: one photograph of a Maxsen home at dusk, with the wordmark small at the bottom. Drawn here with your evening living room photo.

Carpentry wall

  • Four panels, about 1,190 × 1,800 × 150 mm, plywood on a timber frame with a champagne laminate face. They bolt to each other and to the lightbox frame, which keeps the back L standing. A 10 mm dark shadow gap between panels makes the joins look intentional.
  • Each panel has one 800 × 500 mm niche, 40 mm deep, with Lumi LED strip hidden in its top edge. You see the glow on the products, never the light fitting. The niche back lifts out for transport.
  • The wall stands on a 50 mm base set back from the face, with an LED line under it, so it seems to float on light.
  • C4's lower face is a flush, lockable cupboard for bags, brochures and spares. The other lower panels open for wiring and the gateway.
The corner stand

One two-sided stand, angled across the front corner

One 1,000 mm stand at 45° does the work of two: its aisle face looks out at the corner, so people in both aisles see it, and its inside face looks back at the tables. It floats on the same lit base as the wall, over a flush 600 mm steel plate, fully inside the booth line. There is no canopy.

  1. Name, 1,400–1,800The product name in the Maxsen serif, and no other text on the face.
  2. Lit niche, 850–1,350800 × 500, 40 mm deep, with Lumi LED strip hidden in the top edge. The back is an 18 mm ply board cut for 86-type back boxes; it lifts out with four thumb screws.
  3. Plain face, 50–850Plain porcelain fabric. It zips off to reach the power strip and cables.
  4. Floating base, 0–50Set back 40 mm from each face, with a warm LED line that washes the floor.

Lighting

Warm, low light you control

Three layers, all warm: the niches light the products, the LED line under the wall and stand lights the floor, and the dimmed lightbox lights the seating. Keep everything at about 2,700 to 3,000 K; the hall's light is cold and flat, and warm light is what makes people step in. Programme these scenes on the gateway and put them on the S8 and S10 panels.

Three power points and one network

Visitor journey

From the aisle to a booked visit

  1. NoticeThe lit screens on the corner stand catch both aisles at once. The lightbox glows behind.
  2. TouchOn the product wall, visitors press a Lusano key and its niche brightens. They turn Halo and watch C2 warm up.
  3. SitA consultant takes them to a table, opens the Smart Life app on the iPad and picks finishes from the tray.
  4. Leave with a planThe package menus stand on the tables, and the smart lock face looks back at them. Visitors scan the QR or save the WhatsApp number to book a showroom visit or a site check.

Two people on the booth

  • Host at the corner stand: greets from both aisles, demos the panels and the locks, brings people in.
  • Consultant at the tables: walks the product wall with visitors, then sits down to price a package.
  • At busy times, a third person stays on the product wall.

Keep on hand

  • Spare switches for each finish on the wall, in the C4 cupboard.
  • Nothing on the floor or tables except the iPad, finish tray, flowers and menu. Bags and boxes go in the cupboard.
  • A charged power bank for each iPad.
  • Microfibre cloths: crystal and glass faces show fingerprints.
